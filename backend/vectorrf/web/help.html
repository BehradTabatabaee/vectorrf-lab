USER GUIDE

VectorRF Lab

VectorRF Lab is a local, browser-based RF and DSP laboratory. Every instrument works on complex-baseband samples, either simulated by the Transmitter or read from an I/Q Recording. There is no hardware RF front end.

Fast path: the Transmitter starts with a 50 kBd QPSK carrying a PN15 sequence (or open a file in Recordings): look at it in Spectrum, demodulate it in Receiver, or ask the Classifier what it is. Radar Studio is a separate simulation with its own waveform engine.

Captures

Instruments observe numbered captures. Transmitter capture k starts at absolute sample k·N (N samples per capture), so consecutive captures join without gaps: carrier phase, hop schedules, bursts and analog messages continue across them. Every observer naming the same capture sees identical samples.

Pacing

The Pacing switch in each Source section decides how the transmitter stream advances. Real time (default) follows the wall clock: every update shows the capture at the current time and skips whatever could not be processed, like an SDR dropping buffers, so hops and bursts appear at their true rate at any sample rate. Every sample processes each capture in order, so a processing chain's filter memory and loop state are physically continuous; simulated time then runs slower than real time when processing cannot keep up. A receiver or processing chain never sees the same capture twice. Recordings follow their own playback settings.

Working with the interface

Each workspace has a control dock on the left and its displays on the right. The status bar shows the live source, its sample rate, the achieved display rate and the latest message; validation errors appear there and in a banner, and the display keeps the last valid capture.

Typing values

Numeric fields accept instrument notation: 2.4G, 2.4 GHz, 100k, 20us, 1e6 or −3 dBm. m is milli and M mega, except in frequency, sample-rate and symbol-rate fields, where prefixes work as typed: 10mhz is 10 MHz and 2.4ghz is 2.4 GHz. Press Enter or leave the field to apply; ↑/↓ step the value (Shift × 10). Values outside a field’s limits are clamped and the limit is shown.

Where a source has an RF centre, frequency fields show absolute frequencies: type 920M and the baseband offset from the centre is stored.

A value the field cannot take — a frequency beyond the sampled band, for example — is not applied: the field turns red with the allowed range (and, for a frequency, the sample rate that would hold it) and a notice appears. The steppers and arrow keys move a frequency in steps of a hundredth of the sample rate's decade, so a carrier at 0 Hz moves visibly.

Help in context

Labels with a dotted underline explain themselves when you hover over them. Sections collapse and remember their state.

Transmitter

Choose a waveform: tones, multitone, noise, chirps and custom (expression, control points, I/Q table or a local Python module); PSK/QAM/APSK; FSK/MSK/GMSK; OFDM with an editable resource grid; analog AM/FM/PM/SSB/DSB-SC. Tabs separate the signal, its data and framing, OFDM shaping, hopping/sweep and burst gating.

The dock sets the clock (sample rate and capture length, bounded in samples), the RF centre, power, frequency offset, phase and transmitter impairments (AWGN, I/Q imbalance, DC). The right-hand column shows the live output, the nominal band plan and the hop/sweep schedule.

RF centre

The RF centre is the frequency complex-baseband 0 Hz represents. It labels instrument axes, travels into SigMF exports as the capture frequency, and lets every frequency field be entered as an absolute value. With 0 Hz the lab works in plain baseband.

Composite signals

Switch the Transmitter to Composite to sum up to 16 independently designed signals, for example a CW tone at 0 Hz and a 10 MHz-wide QPSK carrier at +20 MHz. Each component is a complete transmitter design with its own waveform, data, absolute power, frequency, phase, burst gating and hopping; the composite adds a common offset, hopping, burst and impairments on top.

Each component’s on-power is calibrated to its own dBm setting: a −10 dBm tone reads −10 dBm and a −20 dBm QPSK integrates to −20 dBm in a channel-power window. Independent components add in power; identical deterministic ones (a duplicated tone at the same frequency) add as amplitudes, +6 dB, and the total power shown accounts for it. Components get their own noise and PN data. Duplicate places the copy at a free frequency, so it never hides on top of its original. The scale is fixed by the design rather than measured per capture, so bursts and hops never step the sample level between captures.

The band plan draws each component’s nominal occupied band at its power, with hop/sweep ranges hatched; red dashed lines are Nyquist. A warning appears when any component would alias.

Hopping and sweeps

Any signal, including a composite and each of its components, can hop or sweep. Frequency hopping visits a linear grid (start → stop in steps), an explicit list, or a seeded pseudo-random permutation of the grid; linear sweep chirps start → stop as a sawtooth or triangle. Dwell (or sweep time), off-time, raised-cosine edge ramps and a start delay complete the schedule.

Example: QPSK hopping from 900 MHz to 1 GHz in 100 kHz steps with 100 µs dwell and 20 µs off-time: set the RF centre to 950 MHz and the sample rate above the 100 MHz span (125 MS/s), enable hopping, and type 900M, 1G, 100k, 100u, 20u. That is 1001 hops with a 120.1 ms period; examples/designs/fhss-qpsk-900m.json holds the design.

Exact phase

The schedule is a 64-bit direct digital synthesizer: every frequency is a tuning word and the phase is an integer accumulator, so the phase of any absolute sample is exact at any rate and after any simulated time, and captures join seamlessly. Continuous phase integrates frequency through every hop (no phase steps, like a VCO). Coherent phase gives each hop the phase its own oscillator would have had since t = 0 (like switched coherent synthesizers). Sweeps are always continuous.

Off-times gate the signal off; transmitter AWGN continues, so the noise floor stays visible. Power reads the configured value while a hop is on. Combining frequencies (component offset + component hop + composite offset + composite hop) must stay within ±Fs/2.

Exporting I/Q

The last design the server accepted is restored when the page reopens. Export I/Q file writes the capture the instruments are showing (same seed and start sample), or a longer contiguous record when a duration is set, as SigMF (recommended: keeps rate, RF centre, the power calibration and the design that made it), WAV/RF64 or raw interleaved ci16/cf32/ci8/cu8/cf64. Integer formats are peak-normalized with headroom or written at unity scale; clipped samples are reported. Exports can be downloaded or saved into the recording library.

Recordings

The library lists I/Q files from SDR tools; drop files anywhere on the page or choose them. Uploads stream to disk and files are memory-mapped, so multi-gigabyte wideband captures open instantly. Requests name files inside the library folder only (VECTORRF_CAPTURES_DIR).

Overview and playback

Selecting a file shows its key properties, a transport bar and a whole-file spectrogram (time downwards) with an aligned power strip and the average spectrum. Click the spectrogram to seek; drag vertically to mark a time region for export. Use as live source sends the recording to Spectrum, Receiver and Classifier. Contiguous playback advances by one window per frame (every sample, usually slower than real time); real time skips samples; custom uses your step. The overview samples the file at evenly spaced segments and states the examined fraction.

Interpretation

Headerless files are ranked by content heuristics; a warning appears when the chosen format contradicts the content, and clicking a candidate applies it. Rate and centre are parsed from gqrx/SDR# file names when possible. Levels read in dBFS (a full-scale complex tone is 0 dBFS); enter an offset for absolute dBm, or restore the calibration stored in a lab export.

Spectrum analyzer

Set centre and span (or start and stop) like a swept analyzer. Spans up to a quarter of the sample rate are digitally down-converted: the analyzer mixes the centre to DC and decimates through Kaiser stages with ≥ 110 dB alias rejection, so a narrow span reaches the RBW the capture length allows instead of the FFT-size limit: at 100 MS/s with a 131,072-point FFT limit the full band bottoms out at 1.1 kHz RBW, while a 400 kHz span is decimated by 128 and reaches 143 Hz from the same 2-million-sample capture. The readout shows the decimation and analysis rate. A span wider than the source rate keeps its axis and shades the region beyond ±Fs/2, labelled OUTSIDE SOURCE NYQUIST.

Resolution, traces and waterfall

Auto RBW keeps RBW at span/500; otherwise type it. RBW is the window’s equivalent noise bandwidth; the window never extends past the captured samples, and when a capture is too short the achieved RBW is reported. Each FFT is zero-padded four times, so a tone reads its level within 0.1 dB and keeps the same shape wherever it falls between bins. The default window is Blackman-Harris (−92 dB sidelobes), close to an analyzer’s Gaussian RBW filter. Four traces can write, hold maxima or minima, average or freeze; memories keep traces for comparison. The waterfall shares the frequency axis, shows the newest row at the top and restarts when you retune.

Markers and measurements

Up to eight markers read frequency and level on any trace. Delta markers read the difference to a reference marker; noise markers read power density (level − 10·log10RBW, valid with the average detector). Marker → CF retunes the analyzer; Marker → Ref sets the reference level.

Channel power

Channel-power windows integrate power over a band from the full-resolution spectrum (not the reduced display): calibrated power per bin divided by the window’s equivalent noise bandwidth in bins, with edge bins weighted by their overlap. Each window also reports power density and its 99 % occupied bandwidth. Add one from a selection, then drag or resize it on the trace.

Zero span and modulation quality

Zero span and modulation quality follow the live stream: each processed capture updates the view, and the readout bar names that capture. Zero span plots I/Q, envelope, unwrapped phase and instantaneous frequency over an interval of the current capture. With tuning on, the record is mixed to a centre frequency, anti-alias decimated and filtered, like an analyzer’s zero-span RBW filter. Tuning bands must fit inside the source Nyquist interval. The readout shows the achieved bandwidth when a short capture cannot resolve the requested one. If real-time pacing shortens a capture, the interval moves into the available samples and the controls show the actual interval. Phase and frequency are masked at signal nulls.

Modulation quality reports decision-directed EVM and MER for linear formats and OFDM with the known transmitter modulation (hopping is removed when the dwells are continuous). Its receiver keeps tracking the carrier from capture to capture and measures the most recent 2,048 symbols (the Record readout), so the constellation fills in and the EVM stays steady even when a capture holds only a few symbols — at 2 GS/s a 50 kBd signal has 40,000 samples per symbol. Signals without symbols show their I/Q trajectory, envelope, instantaneous frequency and phase, with the panes named accordingly.

Receiver

Live monitor shows the processed samples; Demodulation recovers symbols and bits; Framing acquires generic frames; Timing, Carrier and Equalizer focus on each loop; the Processing chain inserts gain, frequency shift, FIR and low-pass filters, resampling, DC removal, AGC, expressions, synchronization blocks and Python extensions.

Following the transmitter

The badge in the dock header shows whether the receiver follows the transmitter (modulation, rates, filters, frame plan, tuning) or is manual. Editing a format field switches to manual with a notice; “follow TX” in the badge re-synchronizes. For a composite, choose which component to receive. For hopping or swept signals the receiver removes the known schedule before demodulating, like a synchronized frequency-hopping receiver.

Synchronization

Symbols are normalized to unit RMS before the loops, so loop gains mean the same thing at any signal level and samples per symbol; the Carrier section shows the resulting damping, natural frequency, lock-in range, noise bandwidth and settling time. Coarse CFO acquires carrier offsets up to about ±10 % of the symbol rate: the power spectrum’s centroid arbitrates M-th power estimates, which are precise but ambiguous (and fooled by short repeating payloads); APSK and differential formats get estimators of their own. Without it, the carrier loop alone pulls in offsets of about its lock-in range (shown in the Carrier section) and beyond that the constellation keeps rotating. Mueller & Müller timing is decision-directed, so it runs jointly with the carrier loop and decides on carrier-corrected symbols; Gardner timing is decision-free and is used for the differential formats and OQPSK. Between captures the receiver keeps tracking (also across captures real time skipped) instead of acquiring again. The timing loop gain is in symbols per unit of detector output, so it too behaves the same at any oversampling.

Linear modulations are demodulated at no more than 16 samples per symbol: higher oversampling (a 50 kBd signal at 10 MS/s has 200) is first reduced by a ≈100 dB anti-alias polyphase resampler, so the cost follows the number of symbols rather than the sample rate and results do not depend on it.

The channel experiment adds SNR, carrier frequency and phase offset, fractional timing delay, a delayed echo and I/Q imperfections; switch it off to analyse a recording as recorded. The Before carrier recovery panes show the symbols with the channel’s phase still on them: with a carrier offset they turn, which is what the loop removes.

Equalizer

The CMA equalizer is a blind, symbol-spaced adaptive filter for PSK; it keeps its taps from capture to capture and converges over a few captures. Its taps are plotted with the channel estimated against the known transmitted symbols (BPSK, QPSK, 8-PSK and QAM transmitters): add an echo in the channel experiment to see both.

Classifier

The classifier is a heuristic observer of one emitter. Drag across an emitter in its spectrum and choose Classify this band, or pick a detected emitter (green brackets): the band is mixed to 0 Hz, decimated and low-pass filtered before any feature is measured, and its SNR is measured against the capture’s noise floor.

Why a class

The Decision path lists every rule in order, each feature measured against its threshold with pass or fail, and the stage that decided. Features shows each value against the thresholds the rules use, with definitions. Constellation hypotheses lists the fit EVM of every linear constellation and the fraction of its points the data used: random data visits all points, so a hypothesis that leaves many unused is penalized. Scores are heuristic agreement, not calibrated probabilities.

Radar Studio

Radar Studio simulates point targets, a receive array, propagation and processing from one sample clock: the waveform design, echoes and processing all run at the simulation sample rate (up to 5 GS/s). The transmit waveform can be a pulse sequence, a continuous waveform (CW, FMCW, PMCW, stepped CW), segments, an arbitrary waveform, the Transmitter’s OFDM design, or a recording window.

Waveform designer

The sequence timeline shows items to scale; click an item to edit it. For the selected pulse item the designer reports PRF, duty, bandwidth, pulse-compression ratio, range resolution, blind range, unambiguous range and velocity, and velocity resolution at the RF carrier.

Scene

The scene is a top view with the array at the bottom centre, boresight up and +Y to the left. Range rings, bearings and the live array pattern are drawn; targets show velocity vectors (20 s of motion) and trails. Drag a target to move it, drag its arrow tip to set its velocity, double-click empty space to add one. The gold square is the strongest-cell position estimate; red arcs are detected ranges.

Processing offers full-waveform delay/Doppler correlation (any waveform), pulse-Doppler and FMCW processors with MTI clutter cancellation and an editable processing chain.

Detection

Detectors compare each range–Doppler cell with a noise level estimated from training cells on both sides along range, outside guard cells, scaled for the false-alarm probability you set: CA-CFAR averages the training cells (best in uniform noise), GO-CFAR takes the greater side (holds the false-alarm rate at clutter edges), SO-CFAR the smaller side (resolves close targets) and OS-CFAR a chosen rank of the sorted cells (tolerates other targets among the training cells); or set a fixed threshold. A target lifts several cells around its peak; each group of cells above threshold is one detection, listed with its range, velocity and margin above the threshold. Narrowband far-field point targets and a stop-and-hop approximation within a capture are assumed.

Power and units

Transmit power is an absolute simulator calibration: it sets the clean signal’s power, with AWGN, DC and I/Q imbalance adding on top. A bin-centred tone reads its configured dBm at the peak; modulated signals spread the same power over their bandwidth (measure it with channel power). Bursts and hops read their configured power while on. Tones read within 0.1 dB at any frequency; the flat-top window is the most accurate for amplitudes. Recordings read dBFS unless calibrated.

Wideband operation

Sample rates up to 5 GS/s are supported. The engine bounds samples per capture (8,388,608 by default, VECTORRF_MAX_CAPTURE_SAMPLES), so a capture spans less time at higher rates. The live real-time setting uses still shorter windows, advances with wall time and skips samples it cannot process; Every sample processes complete consecutive captures even when simulated time falls behind wall time. All DSP works in samples and symbols with exact integer-sample carrier and DDS phase, so a GS/s experiment reproduces the same experiment at MS/s with frequencies scaled.

Troubleshooting

A field turns red

The text is not a quantity in that field’s unit (try forms like 2.4G or 1e6), or the value is outside what the field can take: the message gives the allowed range. A frequency beyond ±Fs/2 needs a higher sample rate.

A design is rejected

The message names the path of the offending setting, for example “components → 1 → center_hz”. Frequencies plus hopping must stay within ±Fs/2.

A recording looks like noise or reads absurd levels

Its sample format is probably wrong: raw files store none. Pick the format the content heuristics suggest or the one your SDR tool documents.

Decoded text is garbled

Check that the receiver follows the transmitter (badge), that coarse CFO is on for large offsets, and the EVM trace for loss of lock; QPSK carrier recovery has a fourfold phase ambiguity without a known preamble.

The engine is unreachable

Keep the launch terminal open; the default address is http://127.0.0.1:8765 (VECTORRF_PORT changes it).

Verifying an installation

Run python -m pytest in the project directory, and python tests/ui/ui_smoke.py for the browser test.
